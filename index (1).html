Upstream Price Pressures and Consumer Inflation: Testing Supply Chain Price Transmission in the U.S.

Research focus. Investigated whether price pressures originating upstream in the supply chain — at the producer and import stage — systematically lead the prices U.S. consumers pay, connecting classical supply chain theory (the bullwhip effect) with macro-empirical evidence on inflation.

Data & methods. Analyzed U.S. Consumer Price Index, Producer Price Index (Final Demand), and Import Price Index data (BLS, Jan 2015–Jul 2026) using cross-correlation analysis and pairwise Granger-causality F-tests across multiple lag structures, implemented in Python (NumPy/SciPy).

p = 0.021 import prices → CPI, 1-month lag
one-way CPI never Granger-causes import prices

Key finding. Import prices Granger-cause CPI changes without CPI ever Granger-causing import prices in return — a one-directional relationship consistent with import prices acting as a genuinely exogenous, upstream supply-chain shock. Producer and consumer prices show strong bidirectional causality, reflecting a tightly coupled domestic feedback loop.

Tools. Python, NumPy, Pandas, SciPy, Matplotlib

Insurance Fraud Detection Using Machine Learning

Research focus. Investigated the use of machine-learning classification methods to identify potentially fraudulent insurance claims and support data-driven risk assessment.

Data & methods. Performed exploratory data analysis, feature preprocessing, class-imbalance handling using SMOTE, and model evaluation across multiple classification approaches, including Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, Support Vector Machine, and neural-network methods.

Key insight. Compared model performance and generalization across training, validation, and test results while examining the challenges of class imbalance and overfitting in fraud detection.

Tools. Python, Pandas, Scikit-learn, SMOTE, TensorFlow/Keras, Matplotlib, Seaborn

Amazon Sales & Customer Sentiment Analysis

Research focus. Examined how product pricing, discounts, ratings, and customer sentiment interact within Amazon marketplace data to better understand consumer responses and product-level demand patterns.

Data & methods. Analyzed 1,465 Amazon products using exploratory data analysis, data preprocessing, regression modeling, and natural language processing. Customer review text was evaluated using sentiment polarity and subjectivity analysis.

Key insight. Structured product attributes and unstructured customer-review text can be combined to generate data-driven insight for pricing and demand decisions.

Tools. Python, Pandas, NumPy, Scikit-learn, Matplotlib, Seaborn, TextBlob

Predicting Global Life Expectancy Using Machine Learning

Research focus. Investigated how socioeconomic and health indicators are associated with life expectancy and evaluated statistical and machine-learning approaches for predicting it across countries.

Data & methods. Analyzed global health indicators including adult mortality, GDP, schooling, alcohol consumption, BMI, HIV/AIDS prevalence, and income composition. Compared multiple regression, decision tree, and random forest models.

Key finding. Random forest produced the strongest predictive performance, with an R² of approximately 0.96, outperforming the decision tree and regression approaches.

Tools. Python, Pandas, Scikit-learn, Matplotlib, Seaborn, Statistical Analysis
